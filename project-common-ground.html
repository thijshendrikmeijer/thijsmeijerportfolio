Common Ground – Graduation Thesis

A graduation project on what binds a divided city together, explored with a research through design approach.

Client / ContextAcademy of Architecture Amsterdam
TypeAcademic project
WhenFeb 2025 – Dec 2026 (expected)
LocationBellville, Cape Town, South Africa
CategoriesUrban design, Urban planning, Mapping, Participatory design

Overview

Bellville, Cape Town’s second Central Business District, is shaped by realities that exist side by side: formal and informal trade, malls and street vendors, MyCiTi buses and minibus taxis, wealth and poverty often separated by a single road or rail line. My thesis does not treat these dualities as problems to solve, but as parallel and legitimate systems. The research question is: How can urban design translate the intelligence of Bellville’s contrasting realities into visible spatial strategies that create adaptable urban environments?

Through research by design, including fieldwork in Cape Town, I analysed the systems of Bellville. From this I developed four perspectives for Bellville along two axes, Global vs Local and Destination vs Interchange. Each perspective images Bellville in a different future. Rather than choosing one of these perspectives as the outcome for Bellville, the vision presents how you can layer urban interventions from each perspective in time.

Graduation committee: Tess Broekmans (mentor), Eric Frijters, Martin Sobota.
